## ADR-004 — Interior eye navigation with scoped keyboard controls (2026-09-11)

- status: accepted
- context: Part 0 needs a way to walk inside one chamber, the 5.00 × 3.40 m clear base shared by the master and kids bedrooms, with an eye-level camera. ADR-002 deferred keyboard and single-pointer camera control to this navigation work. The interior controls have to meet WCAG 2.1.1 Keyboard, 2.1.4 Character Key Shortcuts (single-character shortcuts must be scoped, remappable or able to be turned off) and 2.5.7 Dragging Movements.
- decision: In the interior view, W A S D move (forward, back, strafe left, strafe right), J L turn left and right, and I K look up and down. Keys are bound by physical `KeyboardEvent.code`, so they keep their position on any keyboard layout. There is no pointer lock and no mouse-drag look, so the interior needs no dragging movement. The keys are scoped to the canvas wrapper, a focusable `role="application"` region named "Interior 3D view" (distinct from the "Interior view" toggle button, so voice control commands are unambiguous). The HUD shows the short hint "Move: W A S D · Look: I J K L"; the region's accessible description spells out each key, states that keys follow physical QWERTY positions, and says that Tab moves to the view toggle. It takes focus when the interior view opens, shows a visible focus outline, and handles keys only while it has focus, which satisfies 2.1.4 (shortcuts active only when the component has focus). Ctrl, Alt and Meta combinations are ignored, and held keys clear when focus leaves the region, the window blurs, or Meta (Cmd) is pressed or released (macOS browsers send no keyup for other keys while Cmd is held). Movement is the pure domain function `stepEyePose`: walk 1.4 m/s, turn 90°/s, look 60°/s, frame step capped at 0.1 s, diagonal movement normalised, position clamped to the clear rectangle shrunk by a 0.25 m body radius, pitch limited to ±80°. Each entry starts in the +x/+z corner of that rectangle facing the opposite corner, so two walls, the corner, floor and ceiling are in view. It is unit-tested without WebGL. The per-frame pose lives in a React ref inside `EyeCameraControls` (Euler order `YXZ`), not in the Zustand store. The chamber uses uniform 0.20 m walls as a deliberate demo simplification: brief §2 "Wall specification" and the drawing use 0.30 m for exterior and void-facing walls, and the full floor model in Part 1 replaces the base chamber spec. The rectangle clamp is replaced by wall collision in Part 3. On-screen hold-to-act buttons are not built yet (Part 3).
- alternatives: arrow keys (they conflict with screen reader browse keys and page scrolling, and one hand cannot combine moving and looking as easily as with two letter clusters); pointer-lock mouse look (needs a pointer, is hostile to keyboard and assistive technology users, and relies on continuous drag-like pointer movement); keeping the pose in the Zustand store (every subscriber would re-render on every frame).
- consequences: ADR-002 is only partly addressed. The interior view is keyboard operable; the exterior orbit is still pointer-only, and single-pointer (button) controls for every camera movement stay open until Part 3, whose ADR supersedes ADR-002. ADR-002 therefore stays accepted. Pointer-only users can switch to the interior view but cannot move in it until the Part 3 buttons exist. Letter keys act only while the view has focus, so after using the HUD a user returns focus to the view before walking. The 0.20 m walls and the single-rectangle clamp are temporary geometry that Parts 1 and 3 replace. The eye pose is not shared state, so a future HUD element that shows the position (for example a minimap) needs a deliberate way to read it. The vertical sizes in `FLOOR_HEIGHTS` (floor-to-floor 3.00 m, wall 2.70 m, door 2.10 m, railing 1.10 m, eye 1.60 m) are provisional: they come from the roadmap, not from the owner brief, which gives no vertical sizes, and the owner confirms them in Part 1 of `TASKS.md` ("confirm unified heights").

## ADR-003 — Sources of truth for the floor model (2026-09-11)

- status: accepted
- context: The 3D floor is built from two owner-authored files that overlap and do not always agree: `docs/house-design-brief.md`, the owner's written requirements (site and orientation, wall specification, room program with exact inner dimensions, open-air elements, door schedule, fixtures, area verification, open items), and `docs/source-of-truth-n-floor.drawio.html`, a draw.io drawing whose pages include earlier layouts. Domain data, tests and geometry need one rule for which file and which page decides a value, and neither file says which one wins.
- decision: The brief wins over the drawing on any conflict. In the drawing, Page-2 is authoritative and Page-4 is history only; drawing scale is 30 px = 1 m. Page-2 gives an envelope of 22.50 × 10.00 m, exterior walls of 0.30 m and interior partitions of 0.20 m (per its legend text), matching brief §2 "Wall specification". The house is modelled as one storey (brief §1 "Site & orientation"): the stairs and elevator are space reserved for future floors, not a second level. No windows are modelled yet, because their size and sill height await owner answers.
- alternatives: the drawing wins (it is precise geometry, but it is an earlier artefact and the brief's §10 "History of changes" records decisions made after some drawn layouts); resolve each conflict case by case during implementation (untracked guesses would end up hard-coded in domain data); model the latest page of the drawing (Page-4 is superseded history, not the current plan).
- consequences: A value is read from the brief first and taken from Page-2 only where the brief is silent. Conflicts between the brief and the drawing, and gaps in both (for example window size and sill), are resolved by the brief or recorded as owner questions, never guessed. Both files are excluded from Prettier in `.prettierignore` so they stay as the owner authored them; the only change made before the first commit removed a one-line handling note at the top of the brief, which carried no requirement. Windows, and any value that depends on them, stay out of the model until the owner answers.

## ADR-002 — Defer keyboard and single-pointer camera navigation (2026-09-11)

- status: accepted
- context: The placeholder scene moves the camera only through drei `OrbitControls`: drag to orbit, wheel or pinch to zoom. Keyboard users cannot move the camera, which fails WCAG 2.1.1 Keyboard, and orbiting needs a dragging movement with no single-pointer alternative, which fails WCAG 2.5.7 Dragging Movements. Camera navigation is the first real feature to be built, so controls made now for a placeholder box would be reworked once the real navigation model (exterior orbit, interior movement between floors and rooms) exists.
- decision: Defer accessible camera navigation to the camera-navigation feature, which must ship keyboard controls and single-pointer (button) controls for every camera movement before it is done. Until then the canvas carries no misleading role (neither `role="img"`, which hides that it is interactive, nor `role="application"`, which announces keyboard interaction that does not exist), and a visually hidden description states that camera movement is pointer-only for now.
- alternatives: build HUD rotate/zoom buttons and key bindings now (throwaway work before the navigation model is designed); `role="application"` on the canvas (misleads assistive technology users into expecting keyboard support).
- consequences: The placeholder scene is not WCAG 2.2 AA conformant for camera movement until the camera-navigation feature lands. The HUD (view mode toggle) is already keyboard and single-pointer operable.

## ADR-001 — Frontend stack: Vite + React + TypeScript + React Three Fiber (2026-09-11)

- status: accepted
- context: The product is a browser-only 3D building that users explore from the outside and then inside (floors, rooms, kitchen). It needs a declarative scene graph that composes with a regular UI layer (HUD, menus), fast local iteration, unit-testable logic, and an end-to-end check that WebGL actually renders. There is no backend.
- decision: Vite 8 + React 19.2 + TypeScript 6.0; three.js through `@react-three/fiber` 9 and `@react-three/drei` 10; Zustand 5 for shared UI state; Tailwind CSS 4 for the HUD; Leva for a development-only tweak panel; Vitest 5 + Testing Library (jsdom) for unit tests; Playwright (Chromium only) for end-to-end tests; ESLint 10 + Prettier. Code lives in a single feature module `src/features/building` with `domain` (pure TypeScript, no React/three) → `application` (store) → `ui` (components) seams, dependencies pointing inward; environment variables are parsed by a pure function (`src/app/parseAppConfig.ts`) that both the Vite config (fail fast at dev/build start) and the app (`src/app/config.ts`) call. Version caps: `react`/`react-dom` stay on `~19.2.8` because `@react-three/fiber` 9.7 declares the peer range `react >=19 <19.3` (19.3.0 is already published); `typescript` stays on `~6.0.3` because `typescript-eslint` 8.x declares the peer range `typescript <6.1.0` (7.x is already published). `jsdom` stays on `^29.1.1` because jsdom 30 requires Node `^22.22.2 || ^24.15.0 || >=26.0.0`, while 29.x accepts Node `^22.13.0 || >=24.0.0` and works with Vitest 5 (peer range `*`). `engines.node` is `^22.13.0 || ^24.0.0 || >=26.0.0`: the intersection of every `engines.node` range in the resolved dependency graph, bounded by Vitest 5 (`^22.12.0 || ^24.0.0 || >=26.0.0`) and ESLint 10 / jsdom 29 (`^20.19.0 || ^22.13.0 || >=24`).
- alternatives: plain three.js (imperative scene management, no component model shared with the HUD); Babylon.js (heavier runtime, weaker React integration); Next.js (server rendering adds nothing to a client-side WebGL app); Redux Toolkit (more ceremony than Zustand for small UI state); Jest (separate transform pipeline, whereas Vitest reuses the Vite config); Cypress (Playwright is faster headless and simpler to run in CI); latest React 19.3 / TypeScript 7 (outside the peer ranges of `@react-three/fiber` and `typescript-eslint`); jsdom 30 (narrows the supported Node range for no benefit to these tests).
- consequences: React and TypeScript upgrades are blocked until `@react-three/fiber` and `typescript-eslint` widen their peer ranges; the `~` ranges keep `pnpm update` from crossing the caps. Node 22 older than 22.13.0 is unsupported; moving to jsdom 30 later raises the floor to `^22.22.2 || ^24.15.0 || >=26.0.0`. The WebGL `<Canvas>` cannot run under jsdom, so the 3D scene is covered only by the Playwright smoke test. Leva is part of the production bundle (its panel is hidden outside development). three.js makes the main chunk large; code splitting is deferred until real building content exists.
