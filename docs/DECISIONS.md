## ADR-013 — Exploring the whole floor: one walk field, a walk that routes itself, a camera flight, and a keyboard-operable exterior (2026-09-12)

- status: accepted, and it supersedes ADR-002
- context: Part 2 rendered the whole floor while letting the viewer walk in one room of it. Walking was clamped to the master bedroom by `interimWalkArea.ts`, which ADR-008 introduced as an interim and promised would be deleted rather than extended, and the whole floor was therefore visible with invisible walls across every doorway. The exterior view had no keyboard controls at all and, precisely because of that, deliberately carried no role and no tab stop: ADR-002 deferred accessible camera navigation to the navigation work and said in as many words that the work must ship keyboard controls and single-pointer controls for every camera movement before it is done. ADR-008 closed the single-pointer half for the interior with the remote control and left the exterior orbit on drag and wheel, recording that the Part 3 decision would be the one to supersede ADR-002. That is this entry. Three further things forced decisions rather than following from the clamp's removal. Knowing where you are stops being free once you can leave a room, so the floor needs a room readout, a way to ask to be taken somewhere, and a map. Cutting between an orbit position tens of metres out and an eye inside a bedroom tells the viewer nothing about how the two are related, so the switch between views needs a camera travel, and a travel needs an answer for viewers who ask for less motion. And the owner asked for one thing no task line had spelled out: choosing a room must **walk** the viewer there rather than teleport them, which turns a menu into a route-finding and steering problem.
- decision: Collision is one walk field, swept once at module load, with three predicates and no special cases. `domain/collision.ts` classifies every cell of the plot by sweeping the sorted unique faces of the plot, the slabs and the wall pieces — the technique `walls.ts` already uses, exact because every face is on the centimetre grid — against a body span of 0 to `PERSON_SPEC.height`: a cell is **floor** where a box's top is the finished floor level, which takes in every slab and every door threshold block; **solid** where a box's vertical extent overlaps the body span, which takes in full walls, parapets and window sill blocks but never a threshold (its overlap is exactly zero) nor a lintel (2.10 to 2.70 m, above the head); and **fall** where it is neither, which is the two side-B voids and the 6.00 m² stair shaft. The headline rule of the part — movement crosses a wall at a port and nowhere else, never through a window — therefore **falls out of the geometry and is written nowhere**: `collision.ts` imports nothing from `domain/ports/`, and a door is passable because its threshold is floor at body height while a window is not because its sill is solid there. This is also why the walkable area is not the slab union, which is what `TASKS.md` originally asked for: the slabs stop at the wall faces, so inside a doorway there is no slab at all, and taking the union would have needed a hand-built bridge rect per door, filtered to "door, not window" — exactly the special rule the exit condition forbids. A fall edge stops the body like a wall does, a body radius short of the brink (0.25 m, and 0.275 m at the two railed balcony edges), so the viewer cannot lean out over a shaft to look down; consistency with every other obstacle won over the view. Movement resolves x then z, each axis swept rather than sampled, so an arbitrarily large step cannot tunnel a 0.15 m wall, and `stepEyePose` remains the single mover with `MovementIntent` as plain data. The automatic walk is not a second mover but an intent source competing with the keyboard and the pad for that one call: it returns signs, collision decides what happens, and it learns the outcome from the next frame's pose, which is exactly why it cannot cross a wall and why being stuck is its only failure mode. Its route extends `reachability.ts` rather than adding a second graph — the existing breadth-first walk is factored into one private traversal returning a predecessor map, `getReachableSpaceIds` becomes its key set with its contract, messages and whole test file unchanged, and `findSpaceRoute` walks the predecessors back — because a second derivation of the same edge rule is the one-detector mistake ADR-012 exists to warn about. Waypoints come from `getSlabs` rather than from `space.rects`, so the stair flights are excluded without being named, with two waypoints per crossing (one each side, because a 0.30 m wall is deeper than a 0.50 m body and a single point in the opening would have the walker turning while straddling the jambs), the crossing derived from the **contact** rather than from `getPortOpening` (which throws for the zero-gap `stairs ↔ corridor` join, and where a pair has two such contacts the widest span wins, z 4.75 rather than 5.75), and a destination taken as the centre of the largest **standing** rect — area shrunk by the body on both axes — rather than of the bounding box or of the largest rect by plain area. Both alternatives are demonstrably wrong on this floor: the bounding-box centre of the stairs, (3.60, 5.00), is inside a flight and therefore a hole, and the guest room's north strip wins on plain area, 6.08 against 4.34 m², while being only 0.75 m deep, so plain area would land "Go to guest room" in the circulation strip instead of the room; standing area puts it at (5.50, 7.825). Steering is turn-then-go with hysteresis and cross-track correction against the leg **line** rather than the leg's end point, because arcing into a 0.90 m door with 0.20 m of slack is how a walker scrapes a jamb, and every tolerance is derived from `EYE_NAVIGATION_CONFIG` rather than written down. Stuck detection is time-based rather than frame-based, so a 144 Hz machine is not given a quarter of the patience of a 30 Hz one, and after 1.5 s without progress the walk is abandoned and announced rather than re-planned: nothing on this floor moves, so a block is a geometry defect worth surfacing, and re-planning from a wedged pose invites a loop. Waypoint arrival is decided by an **along-track foot test** and not by distance alone, which is the one defect measurement found and forced a change of rule rather than of constant. Checking "am I within `arrivalRadius`" once per frame can step straight over the arrival disc: a walker drifting at the full lateral deadband crosses that disc on a chord of 0.1375 m while one worst-case step is `walkSpeed × maxStepSeconds` = 0.1400 m, which is wider. Measured, **4 of 300 walks were blocked at dt = 0.1 s against 0 of 1800 at frame times from 1/30 to 1/240**, and one traced failure came within 0.4 mm of the radius, missed it, and — because the bearing is frozen at the leg's origin, so the walker never re-aims once past a waypoint — sailed 1.87 m onward and abandoned the walk. This is not an exotic case: `dt` is capped at 0.1 s, so any frame slower than 10 fps pins it at the cap, and the interior view runs at roughly 2 fps under software WebGL, which is the very environment the required end-to-end walk runs in. Raising the radius cannot fix it, and that is the part worth remembering: at the cap a single diagonal step moves 0.099 m sideways, already wider than the arrival radius itself, so **no radius sized for a nominal frame can guarantee sampling at the worst-case frame** and only a geometric test can. A waypoint is now reached when the distance is within `arrivalRadius` **or** the projection of the pose minus the leg origin onto the leg's bearing has passed the leg's length, which is monotone in progress and therefore cannot be stepped over at any frame rate; after the fix, **0 of 2700 walks were blocked** across the same frame times including the cap. The exterior view becomes keyboard-operable and gains an on-screen pad, which is what closes ADR-002. `domain/orbitNavigation.ts` owns a spherical `OrbitPose` stepped per frame with the arrow keys orbiting and tilting and `Equal`/`Minus` plus their numpad twins zooming, chosen so nothing overlaps `EYE_KEY_BINDINGS` or `KeyV`, asserted by a test; drei's `OrbitControls` keeps pointer drag and wheel, because driving it by keyboard is a dead end — its own key handling _pans_, which would destroy the derived framing pivot, and rotate and tilt have no imperative API. The limits come from `useExteriorFraming`, so the domain clamp and the controls' clamp are the same numbers and cannot fight. The six orbit actions each get a hold-to-act button, and rather than hand-copying the roughly 120 lines of anti-stuck logic in `RemoteControl.tsx` — a pointer ledger and four release nets, where a stuck turn spins the camera forever — that logic was extracted into a generic `createHoldToActStore` and a shared `ui/HoldPad.tsx`, with both pads as thin wrappers; the extraction landed as a pure refactor, with `RemoteControl.test.tsx` and the three existing `remoteControl.spec.ts` tests unchanged and green, before `OrbitPad` was added. The exterior camera now **remembers its orbit angle** across exterior, interior and back, in `application/exteriorOrbitStore.ts`, as a target-relative pose rather than a raw position, so it survives a canvas resize where a position would not, written at three moments only — the end of a pointer drag, the frame a held key or pad button is released, and the component's unmount, which is the write that makes the memory work at all. The `hasOrbited` latch of ADR-008 is **deleted outright**: its job was to avoid overwriting the viewer's view, and a remembered pose cannot overwrite itself. The view switch flies the camera over `VIEW_TRANSITION_SECONDS` = 0.9 s of eased travel, owned by one `useFrame` in `ui/ViewTransition.tsx` mounted for the whole life of the scene **outside** the view ternary, with the ternary's two control components not mounted while a flight runs, because both write the camera every frame and would simply overwrite the interpolation. The start pose is read off the live camera, so there is no jump whatever the previous control left behind, and the hand-off rule is one sentence: the flight's endpoint expression and the mounting control's placement expression must be the same expression, which is why the exterior endpoint and `ExteriorCameraControls`' placement both read the remembered orbit pose and the interior endpoint and the explorer's own pose both come from the single `INTERIOR_START_POSE` object in `ui/floorInstance.ts` rather than from two equal computations. `prefers-reduced-motion` governs **that flight and nothing else**: under the setting the phase never leaves `'none'` and the switch is bit-for-bit the instant one the app had before flights existed, while an automatic walk is untouched in speed, route and outcome, because walking is the content and not decoration; one test pins exactly that distinction rather than a global motion switch. Interior entry always starts at the stairs arrival, (5.10, 5.00) facing the corridor, with no remembered interior pose, so the interior baseline keeps depending on one fixed pose rather than on history. Awareness is carried by the matricule. `Space` gains a required `matricule` field built in `floorPlanData.ts`, the one module already licensed to read `sourceOfTruth/plan.ts`, and one formatter `getSpaceLabel` yields `R11/KIT · Kitchen` for every consumer, so no second spelling of a room name can exist anywhere in the app, failure messages included; the owner chose matricule plus name over the bare name. The pose reaches the HUD without re-rendering React: the pose itself is a non-reactive module-level value written every frame, only the resolved room id is reactive, and the room is re-resolved after 0.05 m of movement — a distance threshold rather than a time one, so it is deterministic in tests with no clock to fake and does nothing at all while the viewer stands still or turns in place. `domain/currentSpace.ts` keeps the **last known** room whenever `findSpaceAt` returns a void or nothing, which it does constantly for the 0.15 to 0.30 m wall bands a 0.25 m body straddles, so standing in a doorway you are in the room you came from until you are in the next one and the readout never blanks or chatters. The readout is a `<p>` with `aria-live="polite"` and `aria-atomic` and deliberately **no role**, because a second `role="status"` on the page would break the HUD mask of the screenshot specs and every bare `getByRole('status')` query, and it stays mounted, empty and `sr-only` when there is nothing to say, because a live region must be in the DOM before its text changes. A successful walk announces exactly twice, where it is going and where it ended up, with the per-room line suppressed in between so that crossing three rooms does not announce three times. "Go to room" is a disclosure plus native buttons rather than a `<select>` (arrow-keying a closed select fires `change` per key on some platforms, so every arrow press would start a walk) and rather than `role="menu"` or `role="listbox"` (which promise an arrows, Home/End and typeahead contract that native buttons give for free), with the list computed once at module level because the floored plan is one connected component and a list whose items appeared and vanished as the viewer walked would be a focus hazard. Because activating a menu item starts motion the viewer did not physically drive, there are three ways to stop it: an on-screen "Stop walking" button, `Escape` while the view has focus, and any manual movement input at all, look-only included. `Escape` is not a single-character shortcut, so WCAG 2.1.4 is not engaged by it. The minimap is interior-only, since outside the whole floor is already on screen, and its rooms are **pointer-only** targets with the full-size "Go to room" menu as the keyboard and touch route to the same store action, which is the equivalent-control exception WCAG 2.5.8 allows and is the honest answer when a room is about 15 px across at any HUD-sensible size. It is hidden below the `sm` breakpoint because everything it conveys is available as text through the readout and the menu, which is the test of a legitimate responsive `hidden`. Finally, the view region is a focusable `role="application"` in **both** views, named "Interior 3D view" and "Exterior 3D view", each distinct from the toggle that switches to it, and it takes focus on entering the interior and on returning to the exterior but **never on first page load**, so no focus ring is drawn over the building on arrival (owner decision).
- alternatives: keeping the interim one-room clamp and widening it room by room (it would grow into a second, competing collision model, which is what ADR-008 said it must not become); taking the walkable area as the slab union with a per-door bridge rect (it needs a "door, not window" rule the exit condition forbids, and it puts a second hand-written description of the floor beside the swept one); sampling movement per axis instead of sweeping it (a large step then tunnels a thin wall, and the frame-time cap guarantees large steps); letting the body lean out over a fall edge since nothing physical stops an eye there (it makes the void the one obstacle on the floor that behaves differently from every other); teleporting on a room pick, which is what the task line implied (the owner asked for a walk, and a jump teaches nothing about how the rooms connect); a second route graph in its own module (two derivations of one edge rule with one detector each, the exact mistake ADR-012 records); waypoints from `space.rects` rather than from the slabs (the stair flights would then have to be named and excluded by hand); one waypoint per crossing (the walker turns while straddling a 0.30 m jamb); destinations at the bounding-box centre (the stairs' centre is a hole) or at the largest rect by plain area (it lands the guest room's destination in a 0.75 m circulation strip); raising `ARRIVAL_STEP_FACTOR` or the arrival radius to stop the sampled overshoot (measurement shows one diagonal step at the frame cap moves further sideways than the radius itself, so no radius can be large enough without being wider than a cubicle door); declaring the overshoot a test-environment problem and lowering it with a faster `walkSpeed` under test (it would hide the defect in exactly the configuration real viewers on slow machines run); frame-based stuck detection (it scales the walker's patience with the refresh rate); re-planning a blocked walk (a wedged pose invites a loop, and a block is a defect worth surfacing); driving drei's `OrbitControls` from the keyboard (its key handling pans the target, destroying the derived framing pivot, and rotate and tilt have no imperative API); a second hand-written copy of the hold-to-act logic for the orbit pad (a stuck turn spins the camera forever, and ADR-012 exists to warn about exactly this duplication); remembering the exterior camera as a raw position (a resize re-derives the framing target, so a position no longer means the same view while an angle does); keeping the `hasOrbited` latch alongside the memory (its flaw was dying with its component, and a remembered pose cannot overwrite itself); a `role="status"` for the room readout (it would break the HUD mask and every bare status query in the specs); a `<select>` or a `role="menu"` for the room list (a change of context per arrow key in the first case, a whole keyboard contract to reimplement in the second); listing only the rooms reachable from where the viewer currently stands (the floored plan is one connected component, so the list would be identical anyway while appearing to shift under the viewer's focus); offering the voids in the list and refusing them (a permanently unusable control is noise in the Tab order and in a screen reader's list of buttons); making the minimap rooms focusable (twenty-two sub-24 px tab stops through a picture, where the equivalent full-size control already exists); one global motion switch covering the flight and the walk alike (it would slow or skip locomotion the viewer asked for, and reduced motion is about decoration); focusing the view region on first load now that the exterior is focusable (a focus ring over the building before the viewer has asked for anything); renaming `INTERIOR_REGION_ID` now that it serves both views (six files across three owners for no change in behaviour); editing ADR-002 and ADR-008 in place to match what was built (an ADR is history, and a supersession is recorded rather than backdated).
- consequences: ADR-002 is superseded and its gap is closed: both views are keyboard operable, both have single-pointer on-screen controls for every camera movement, and the canvas region now carries `role="application"` in both — which ADR-002 explicitly refused while the keyboard support did not exist, and which is only honest now that it does. ADR-008 keeps its text, and two of its promises are deliberately **broken** rather than kept, both in the direction it authorised when it said that whatever Part 3 does not consume gets deleted. `createInitialEyePose`, kept there as ADR-004's corner-pose history, is **deleted** along with the single-room clamp it was built on: ADR-004's own text remains the history, which is what an ADR is for, and a dead function is a worse record than the entry describing it. `getBlockedRects` in `stairs.ts`, which ADR-008 named as speculative and expected Part 3's collision to consume, is **deleted rather than consumed**: the stair shaft has no slab, so the sweep already blocks it, and keeping the export would have been a second hand-written description of the same hole. `getReachableSpaceIds` is the opposite case — the route finder and the reachability invariant test both consume it, so it stops being dead code. `interimWalkArea.ts` and `INTERIM_WALK_SPACE_ID` are gone entirely, as ADR-008 said they would be, and `createRoomCentrePose`, `CameraRoomBox` and `createCameraRoomBox` go with them; `getThirdPersonCamera` keeps its algebra unchanged and takes a camera field instead of a room box, which means the follow camera can now follow through an open doorway instead of being trapped in one convex room. Five costs are accepted and recorded rather than dressed up. The two 0.60 m bath and shower cubicle doors keep only 0.10 m of lateral play, so walking them by hand needs the body within about 5 cm of the centre line; the automatic walk steers through the centre and is unaffected, and the source of truth is deliberately not edited to widen them, because that is not what this part is for. An oblique leg out of an L-shaped room's connector is not body-square-safe in general: the concrete case is the guest room to guest sanitair leg from (5.50, 7.05) to (7.75, 6.75), which clips the notch corner around x 6.9 to 7.1 and z 7.05 to 7.12, and it arises on **no** route from the stairs arrival, which is why the data-wide proof passes; collision still clamps, so the worst outcome is a scrape and possibly a stall-detected abandonment, never a wall crossing, and the fix — approach points either side of a connector — would change waypoint lists that tests now pin, so it is recorded rather than done. Arrival may legitimately land up to one worst-case step, 0.14 m, past a waypoint at the frame-time cap, so the assertion that the final distance is within `arrivalRadius` holds at real frame rates and relaxes by one step at the cap; a destination is a room's standing centre, not a dock. And a **live resize no longer re-fits the building**: placement used to re-derive the camera from the framing on every canvas change, so a resize always fitted the floor, whereas it now keeps the viewer's remembered angle and distance and merely re-clamps into the new limits, which is what remembering the angle means but also means that rotating a desktop window down to phone width can crop the building, since `fitDistance` depends on aspect and the remembered distance does not follow it. A fresh load at any size still fits, because the pose is seeded from that aspect's own framing, so the exposure is limited to a live resize or a device rotation; if it proves to read as broken, the contained fix is a `userAdjusted` flag in the orbit store, set when a pointer or key movement ends and never when seeding, which is not the deleted `hasOrbited` latch, whose flaw was dying with the component. The fifth cost comes with the start pose itself, and it is a framing ADR-008 explicitly rejected: it listed "keeping the corner start pose (third person then opens on a head close-up, in the very view the owner asked for)" among its rejected alternatives, and recorded that the mid-room pose it chose instead "keeps 2.433 m of its 2.50 m follow distance". Putting the viewer where a person actually arrives reintroduces the close view that alternative was rejected for, and the measurement is exact rather than approximate: the arrival landing is 1.00 m deep, so the body centre stands 0.50 m from the stair-shaft fall edge, the 0.15 m camera margin leaves 0.35 m of plan room behind it, and with yaw −π/2 putting the camera's ray at −x, `getThirdPersonCamera(INTERIOR_START_POSE, CAMERA_FIELD)` yields **0.500 m at 45.6° elevation** — `0.35 / cos 45.6°` — which is exactly `minBodyVisibleDistance`, the floor below which the mannequin is hidden. No constant can improve it, because behind the camera is the shaft: it is the building's geometry and not a defect, and the mid-room figure ADR-008 quoted is unreachable now that the pose it described no longer exists. The owner accepted it on 2026-09-12 with the mitigation in front of him: the interior **opens in first person**, so the close view appears only when `V` is pressed while still on the landing, and a couple of steps into the corridor restore the full 2.50 m follow distance. ADR-008 keeps its text here as everywhere else — this entry is the record — and `docs/RUNBOOK.md` now states the measured 0.500 m at 45.6° instead of the 2.433 m it had inherited. One naming debt is taken on knowingly: the view region keeps the id `INTERIOR_REGION_ID` although it now serves both views, because renaming it would touch six files for no behaviour change, and this sentence is the record so the next reader does not mistake it for a bug. The matricule becoming a required field on `Space` is contained, since nothing outside `domain/floorPlan/` constructs a `Space` literal and only three test helpers do; the matricule pattern shipped as `[A-Z]{3,4}` rather than the `[A-Z]{3}` first written down, because `VOID` is the one four-letter type code and the stricter pattern would have thrown on the real plan. Waypoints snap to a half-centimetre grid, because whole centimetres would shift the guest sanitair's approach point from 7.475 to 7.48 and push the body 5 mm toward the far wall of the tightest room in the building, while leaving the value raw reproduces exactly the floating-point noise the plan grid exists to prevent. The interior screenshot baseline is invalid by construction and must be regenerated, since the start pose moves from (4.10, 2.00) to (5.10, 5.00) and the interior HUD grows a readout row, while the exterior baseline **is** replaced on this branch, for a reason that is not a change in the render. Captured without the HUD, this branch and `main` produce a byte-identical exterior frame: nothing about the exterior scene changed in Part 3. The committed baseline was last written before PR #11 rebuilt the floor on the single source of truth, and that PR did not re-record it, so it had been compared against a building that no longer existed — a drift of roughly 5,000 pixels by Playwright's own metric, about 57 % of the 1 % tolerance, which is why the assertion kept passing. Part 3's camera pad then grew the masked HUD band from 80 to 176 px (122,880 px), exhausting the remaining tolerance and finally exposing that older drift: growing the mask did not change the canvas, it removed the headroom that was hiding a whole floor rebuild. The standing guard is kept without the prediction that came with it — the exterior HUD's empty, absolutely positioned live region must contribute **no** pixels, so anything added in flow to the exterior HUD band is a defect — but a moved exterior baseline is no longer evidence of that on its own, and the first question to ask of one is whether it still matches its own commit. "Settled" gains a meaning for the capture helper, which now asserts that no camera flight is pending — the region's `data-camera-transition` — before comparing two identical captures, because a 0.9 s eased flight would almost never survive the old two-captures rule and almost is not a guarantee. In third person the mannequin is absent during the 0.9 s flight back out, because the camera controls are unmounted while the flight owns the camera; it is a known nit, accepted rather than threading a flag through the most contended file of the part. The `prefers-reduced-motion` scope is now a rule to keep rather than a one-off: it governs the view flight and must not be consulted for locomotion, and the test that pins the distinction is what stops a future change from quietly turning it into a global motion switch. One tooling hazard is worth knowing and is recorded in the RUNBOOK: `pnpm typecheck` under-reports on a stale incremental cache — measured at 22 errors from plain `tsc -b` against 78 across 10 files from `tsc -b --force` on the same broken tree — so it still fails the gate correctly, but anyone verifying one file's state should force the build rather than trust the incremental output.

## ADR-012 — Two wall derivations are kept, and their divergence is accepted rather than collapsed now (2026-09-12)

- status: accepted
- context: The floor's walls are derived twice from the one source of truth. `src/features/building/domain/walls.ts` derives them for the 3D model, and `scripts/source-of-truth/walls.mjs` derives them for the generated drawing, and the two are line-for-line twins over most of their length: sixteen functions carry the same names and the same algorithm in both — `near`, `mergeSpans`, `subtractSpans`, `overlapSpan`, `outlineEdges`, `directedEnds`, `orderClockwise`, `facingSpaces`, `wallMatricule`, `backToBack`, `contactsAlong`, `assignIsolationReasons`, `distanceTo`, `nearestContactIndex`, `assignJunctionReasons` and `deriveWalls` — spanning 822 of `walls.mjs`'s 1147 lines, 411 of them non-comment (measured by summing the declaration spans). That duplication was tolerable while it was believed that the pair formed a mutual check on each other, and ADR-010's consequences said so in as many words. It does not: `verify.mjs:35` imports `deriveWalls` from `walls.mjs`, so `pnpm verify:plan` checks 5, 8, 10, 11 and 12 all run on the drawing's own derivation and can never contradict it, while the Vitest suite only ever sees `walls.ts`. Each derivation therefore has exactly one detector, and nothing compares the two against each other. They have already parted. `walls.ts:1225-1230` takes the exterior envelope from the outermost room rects — the minimum and maximum x and z over every rect in the plan — whereas `walls.mjs:926-931` takes it from `PLOT` inset by one exterior thickness. Today those agree, but only by coincidence: the outermost clear rects happen to sit exactly one exterior thickness inside the plot. Two smaller drifts are confirmed in the same pair. The docstring at `walls.mjs:681` states that isolation "only ever upgrades a plain separator: an override or a weather-exposed face already has a stronger reason", while the code it documents, at `walls.mjs:748`, upgrades a weather-exposed stretch too; the `walls.ts` twin at `:900-903` documents the same behaviour correctly. And the two `near()` helpers disagree on tolerance by three orders of magnitude — `walls.ts:319` compares `<= LENGTH_TOLERANCE` (1e-9, `planGeometry.ts:37`) and `walls.mjs:179` compares `< EPS` (1e-6, `walls.mjs:162`).
- decision: Both derivations stay for now, and the divergence is recorded here rather than repaired in this commit. The risk is stated concretely so that it is not rediscovered by accident: the envelope agreement is a coincidence of the current plan, and the first time the owner insets an outermost room — the side-B strip going 0.70 m deep instead of 0.80 m, say — the rect-derived envelope of `walls.ts` moves while the plot-derived envelope of `walls.mjs` does not. The two would then classify different faces as `exterior`, produce different `faces` strings and attach different reasons to the contacts behind them, so the generated drawing and the 3D model would describe different buildings; and neither detector would fire, because `verify:plan` only ever sees `walls.mjs` and the unit suite only ever sees `walls.ts`. Until the collapse happens, a change to either derivation is made to both, and the envelope rule is the first thing to check when a room stops touching the plot edge.
- alternatives: collapsing the duplication now, in this commit (it is estimated at −450 to −500 lines, which is a change of a different kind and a different risk from the documentation fixes this commit carries, and burying it here would make both harder to review and to revert); making `verify.mjs` import `walls.ts` instead so that one derivation serves everything (`node` strips types natively but `verify:plan` must keep running with no toolchain at all, which is exactly what ADR-010 pinned, so this needs checking rather than assuming); having `verify.mjs` import both derivations and diff them, leaving the duplication in place (it would catch the envelope divergence, but it entrenches two copies as a design instead of removing one, and doubles what every future wall change has to be written into); deleting `walls.mjs` outright and generating the drawing from `walls.ts` (that is the collapse, and it is what is being deferred, not rejected); fixing only the three confirmed drifts and leaving the structure alone (it treats the symptoms and leaves the mechanism that produced them, which is that nothing compares the two); saying nothing and leaving the false "two independent implementations" claim standing (it is the claim that made the duplication look like a safety feature, and it is what this entry exists to retract).
- consequences: The governance rule that a code-review finding is either fixed or deferred with an ADR is met by this entry, and the finding is deferred deliberately rather than forgotten. ADR-010 stays accepted with its text unchanged; a parenthetical correction is appended to its consequences, where the "two independent implementations" sentence lives, in the same style ADR-011's closure already uses there, so the original wording survives as the record of what was believed. The same correction is made in `TASKS.md`, `docs/RUNBOOK.md` and `.claude/learning.md`, each of which carried some form of the claim. ## ADR-009 — Deterministic scene lighting: the debug panel owns factors, never intensities (2026-09-12)

- status: accepted
- context: ADR-008 lights the floor with a constant three lights whose intensities differ per view (the interior raises the hemisphere fill and the ambient term, because a roofed interior with no shadow maps receives almost nothing from the sun), and keeps the Leva panel so the owner and developers can tune the scene live. The panel was wired the obvious way: each control was seeded from the intensity of the current view, `getLightingSpec(view)`. That is a trap, and it cost a session to find. Leva never re-seeds a control's _value_: the value is created from its seed the first time the control is rendered, `addData` strips `value` out of the properties it overrides even when a changed dependency array asks it to override, `useValuesForPath` lets the store win over a fresh seed, and `disposePaths` only deletes special inputs, so a plain number input survives unmounting and outlives the component for the whole life of the page. Whichever view rendered first therefore owned the light intensities from then on: entering the interior kept the exterior fill and ambient term, and the very same interior geometry settled into one of two shadings — 167929 differing pixels, a ratio of 0.19 of the frame — decided only by the order the two views had mounted in, with neither state converging to the other however long the scene was left alone. Part 2's exit asks for a rendered baseline of the interior, and a scene with two stable images cannot have one: a baseline taken then would have pinned whichever of the two the generating machine happened to produce.
- decision: The debug panel owns _factors_, never intensities. Every intensity rendered by `ui/SceneLighting.tsx` is `getLightingSpec(view)` times a factor, and every factor is seeded at `NEUTRAL_FACTOR` (1) in both views, so no control is seeded from a view-dependent value and there is nothing for Leva's store to pin; the rendered lighting is a function of `view` alone until a developer drags a control, and the spec of the current view is applied on every view change. Two folders: `Sun` (a factor on the sun plus its elevation and azimuth, both seeded from the constants of `lightingSpec.ts`) and `Sky` (the two hemisphere colours, which are view-independent, plus a factor on the hemisphere fill and one on the ambient term). A factor of 0 darkens a light completely but never removes it, so no control can change `SCENE_LIGHT_COUNT` and none can trigger the shader recompilation ADR-008 avoids. The failure mode is removed by construction rather than repaired after the fact, and `SceneLighting.test.tsx` holds it there: it asserts that every seed is view-independent against a mock that reproduces Leva's store faithfully, including `addData` dropping `value` and the store beating a fresh seed. The general rule this yields, which applies to every Leva control added from here on: a control may only be seeded from a value that does not change for the life of the page. Verified after the fix: six captures of the interior start pose taken across six separate processes and three navigation sequences — the interior entered directly, entered after the exterior had settled, and entered after a round trip out and back — produced one single hash, with all fifteen pairs at 0 differing pixels. That frame is now the committed baseline `tests/e2e/__screenshots__/exterior.spec.ts/interior-first-person-chromium-linux.png`.
- alternatives: re-seeding the controls when the view changes (this is the trap itself, not a fix: `addData` strips `value` on the override path, so the intensities never follow the view); passing a dependency array to the `useControls` schema (same mechanism, same outcome); remounting the panel per view (`disposePaths` keeps plain number inputs, so the value survives the remount anyway, and a toggle would rebuild the whole panel); dropping Leva from the lighting altogether (the panel is what ADR-008 keeps for tuning the scene, and the owner uses it); keeping absolute intensities in the panel and taking the baseline along one fixed navigation path (it pins a bug into the baseline and leaves the running app non-deterministic for the viewer, which is the actual defect); one intensity control shared by both views (the interior needs a higher fill and ambient term than the exterior, ADR-008, and a shared control would flatten one of them).
- consequences: The panel no longer shows absolute intensities: a developer reads a multiplier and looks up the value it multiplies in `lightingSpec.ts`. Because the factors are view-independent by design, a factor dragged while in one view stays applied in the other — correct for a tuning panel, and the reason the interior can no longer be tuned independently from the panel alone; an intensity that should differ per view belongs in `getLightingSpec`, not in a control. Lighting determinism is now a testable property, which is what makes the interior screenshot baseline possible at all. The rule extends to Part 4's lighting pass: shadow-map controls must be seeded from constants too. ADR-008 stays accepted; this refines its lighting decision only and changes nothing else about it.

Each entry starts in the +x/+z corner of that rectangle facing the opposite corner, so two walls, the corner, floor and ceiling are in view. It is unit-tested without WebGL. The per-frame pose lives in a React ref inside `EyeCameraControls` (Euler order `YXZ`), not in the Zustand store. The chamber uses uniform 0.20 m walls as a deliberate demo simplification: brief §2 "Wall specification" and the drawing use 0.30 m for exterior and void-facing walls, and the full floor model in Part 1 replaces the base chamber spec. The rectangle clamp is replaced by wall collision in Part 3. On-screen hold-to-act buttons are not built yet (Part 3).
- alternatives: arrow keys (they conflict with screen reader browse keys and page scrolling, and one hand cannot combine moving and looking as easily as with two letter clusters); pointer-lock mouse look (needs a pointer, is hostile to keyboard and assistive technology users, and relies on continuous drag-like pointer movement); keeping the pose in the Zustand store (every subscriber would re-render on every frame).
- consequences: ADR-002 is only partly addressed. The interior view is keyboard operable; the exterior orbit is still pointer-only, and single-pointer (button) controls for every camera movement stay open until Part 3, whose ADR supersedes ADR-002. ADR-002 therefore stays accepted. Pointer-only users can switch to the interior view but cannot move in it until the Part 3 buttons exist. Letter keys act only while the view has focus, so after using the HUD a user returns focus to the view before walking. The 0.20 m walls and the single-rectangle clamp are temporary geometry that Parts 1 and 3 replace. The eye pose is not shared state, so a future HUD element that shows the position (for example a minimap) needs a deliberate way to read it. The vertical sizes in `FLOOR_HEIGHTS` (floor-to-floor 3.00 m, wall 2.70 m, door 2.10 m, railing 1.10 m, eye 1.60 m) are provisional: they come from the roadmap, not from the owner brief, which gives no vertical sizes, and the owner confirms them in Part 1 of `TASKS.md` ("confirm unified heights").

## ADR-003 — Sources of truth for the floor model (2026-09-11)

- status: accepted
- context: The 3D floor is built from two owner-authored files that overlap and do not always agree: `docs/house-design-brief.md`, the owner's written requirements (site and orientation, wall specification, room program with exact inner dimensions, open-air elements, door schedule, fixtures, area verification, open items), and `docs/source-of-truth-n-floor.drawio.html`, a draw.io drawing whose pages include earlier layouts. Domain data, tests and geometry need one rule for which file and which page decides a value, and neither file says which one wins.
- decision: The brief wins over the drawing on any conflict. In the drawing, Page-2 is authoritative and Page-4 is history only; drawing scale is 30 px = 1 m. Page-2 gives an envelope of 22.50 × 10.00 m, exterior walls of 0.30 m and interior partitions of 0.20 m (per its legend text), matching brief §2 "Wall specification". The house is modelled as one storey (brief §1 "Site & orientation"): the stairs and elevator are space reserved for future floors, not a second level. No windows are modelled yet, because their size and sill height await owner answers.
- alternatives: the drawing wins (it is precise geometry, but it is an earlier artefact and the brief's §10 "History of changes" records decisions made after some drawn layouts); resolve each conflict case by case during implementation (untracked guesses would end up hard-coded in domain data); model the latest page of the drawing (Page-4 is superseded history, not the current plan).
- consequences: A value is read from the brief first and taken from Page-2 only where the brief is silent. Conflicts between the brief and the drawing, and gaps in both (for example window size and sill), are resolved by the brief or recorded as owner questions, never guessed. Both files are excluded from Prettier in `.prettierignore` so they stay as the owner authored them; the only change made before the first commit removed a one-line handling note at the top of the brief, which carried no requirement. Windows, and any value that depends on them, stay out of the model until the owner answers.

## ADR-002 — Defer keyboard and single-pointer camera navigation (2026-09-11)

- status: accepted
- context: The placeholder scene moves the camera only through drei `OrbitControls`: drag to orbit, wheel or pinch to zoom. Keyboard users cannot move the camera, which fails WCAG 2.1.1 Keyboard, and orbiting needs a dragging movement with no single-pointer alternative, which fails WCAG 2.5.7 Dragging Movements. Camera navigation is the first real feature to be built, so controls made now for a placeholder box would be reworked once the real navigation model (exterior orbit, interior movement between floors and rooms) exists.
- decision: Defer accessible camera navigation to the camera-navigation feature, which must ship keyboard controls and single-pointer (button) controls for every camera movement before it is done. Until then the canvas carries no misleading role (neither `role="img"`, which hides that it is interactive, nor `role="application"`, which announces keyboard interaction that does not exist), and a visually hidden description states that camera movement is pointer-only for now.
- alternatives: build HUD rotate/zoom buttons and key bindings now (throwaway work before the navigation model is designed); `role="application"` on the canvas (misleads assistive technology users into expecting keyboard support).
- consequences: The placeholder scene is not WCAG 2.2 AA conformant for camera movement until the camera-navigation feature lands. The HUD (view mode toggle) is already keyboard and single-pointer operable.

## ADR-001 — Frontend stack: Vite + React + TypeScript + React Three Fiber (2026-09-11)

- status: accepted
- context: The product is a browser-only 3D building that users explore from the outside and then inside (floors, rooms, kitchen). It needs a declarative scene graph that composes with a regular UI layer (HUD, menus), fast local iteration, unit-testable logic, and an end-to-end check that WebGL actually renders. There is no backend.
- decision: Vite 8 + React 19.2 + TypeScript 6.0; three.js through `@react-three/fiber` 9 and `@react-three/drei` 10; Zustand 5 for shared UI state; Tailwind CSS 4 for the HUD; Leva for a development-only tweak panel; Vitest 5 + Testing Library (jsdom) for unit tests; Playwright (Chromium only) for end-to-end tests; ESLint 10 + Prettier. Code lives in a single feature module `src/features/building` with `domain` (pure TypeScript, no React/three) → `application` (store) → `ui` (components) seams, dependencies pointing inward; environment variables are parsed by a pure function (`src/app/parseAppConfig.ts`) that both the Vite config (fail fast at dev/build start) and the app (`src/app/config.ts`) call. Version caps: `react`/`react-dom` stay on `~19.2.8` because `@react-three/fiber` 9.7 declares the peer range `react >=19 <19.3` (19.3.0 is already published); `typescript` stays on `~6.0.3` because `typescript-eslint` 8.x declares the peer range `typescript <6.1.0` (7.x is already published). `jsdom` stays on `^29.1.1` because jsdom 30 requires Node `^22.22.2 || ^24.15.0 || >=26.0.0`, while 29.x accepts Node `^22.13.0 || >=24.0.0` and works with Vitest 5 (peer range `*`). `engines.node` is `^22.13.0 || ^24.0.0 || >=26.0.0`: the intersection of every `engines.node` range in the resolved dependency graph, bounded by Vitest 5 (`^22.12.0 || ^24.0.0 || >=26.0.0`) and ESLint 10 / jsdom 29 (`^20.19.0 || ^22.13.0 || >=24`).
- alternatives: plain three.js (imperative scene management, no component model shared with the HUD); Babylon.js (heavier runtime, weaker React integration); Next.js (server rendering adds nothing to a client-side WebGL app); Redux Toolkit (more ceremony than Zustand for small UI state); Jest (separate transform pipeline, whereas Vitest reuses the Vite config); Cypress (Playwright is faster headless and simpler to run in CI); latest React 19.3 / TypeScript 7 (outside the peer ranges of `@react-three/fiber` and `typescript-eslint`); jsdom 30 (narrows the supported Node range for no benefit to these tests).
- consequences: React and TypeScript upgrades are blocked until `@react-three/fiber` and `typescript-eslint` widen their peer ranges; the `~` ranges keep `pnpm update` from crossing the caps. Node 22 older than 22.13.0 is unsupported; moving to jsdom 30 later raises the floor to `^22.22.2 || ^24.15.0 || >=26.0.0`. The WebGL `<Canvas>` cannot run under jsdom, so the 3D scene is covered only by the Playwright smoke test. Leva is part of the production bundle (its panel is hidden outside development). three.js makes the main chunk large; code splitting is deferred until real building content exists.
