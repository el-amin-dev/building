## ADR-008 — Built floor: dog-leg stairs without an elevator, openings, light and an on-screen remote control (2026-09-11)

- status: accepted
- context: Part 2 builds the whole typical floor from the model of ADR-005, which forced the owner to answer what ADR-006 had left as placeholders or open items. ADR-006 showed the vertical circulation as "blocked placeholder volumes (an elevator shaft box and a stair block)" precisely because a straight stair rising 3.00 m does not fit in the 3.70 m bay beside an elevator, and it left open "the owner confirms the walkable arrival area" in front of those volumes; Part 3 needs that point as its interior start pose. Generating solid geometry also raises questions the plan alone cannot answer: how tall a wall is where it faces the open side instead of a room, where each of the 1.20 × 1.20 m windows of ADR-006 actually goes, how the brief §6 door schedule becomes data with the Page-2 offsets, how thick a slab is, how the floor is lit in both views, and how hundreds of boxes are drawn without one draw call each. Two further constraints arrived on 2026-09-11: the owner asked for on-screen controls because a keyboard is not always available to them, and wall collision does not exist until Part 3, so a viewer let loose on the whole floor would walk through every wall.
- decision: No elevator. The owner dropped it, so the bay of brief §4.2 (x 1.60–5.30, z 3.90–5.40) holds stairs alone; the space id and name are now `stairs`, and this partially supersedes ADR-006 on that point. Stairs: a dog-leg with 17 real risers of `floorToFloor / 17` (0.17647… m), a 0.25 m going and a 0.75 m flight width, which is half the 1.50 m depth of the bay. The up-flight occupies the northern half and climbs westward from the landing edge x 4.00 to x 2.00 (tread tops 1 · riser … 8 · riser); the half-landing fills x 1.60–2.00 over the full depth of the bay at riser 9; the return flight occupies the southern half and climbs eastward from x 2.00 back to x 4.00 (tread tops 10 · riser … 17 · riser), the last one exactly `floorToFloor`, the finished level of the next floor. The flight area x 1.60–4.00 is solid and not walkable (`getBlockedRects`); the landing x 4.00–5.30 is walkable and continuous with the corridor through the zero-gap join of brief §4.2, and it carries the door to the link corridor. Arrival: a person arrives at the centre of that landing, (4.65, 4.65), facing the corridor (+x, yaw −π/2), which closes ADR-006's open item; Part 3 raises it to eye height and uses it as the interior start pose. Wall heights: a wall cell rises to `heights.wall` (2.70 m) when either side of it is a room or a circulation space, and only to `heights.railing` (1.10 m), as a parapet, when every side it faces is open air, the void or the outside; a junction cell, the solid where walls meet or a wall dies into a corner, takes the greater height of the classified cells touching it, so a parapet never cuts down the wall it meets. A railing, 0.05 m thick, appears only where a walkable open-air surface meets the void with no wall at all — the two balcony-slab edges, both explicit zero-wall joins — and is a rail, not structure, so it stays outside the 42.52 m² wall footprint of brief §8. Every wall starts at the underside of the slab, `-(floorToFloor - wall)` = −0.30 m, so a cross-section stays closed and a doorway keeps a threshold. Windows: 1.20 × 1.20 m with a 0.90 m sill and a 2.10 m head (the door height, kept as its own field), one per room per glazeable face, centred in that room's longest free run on the side-A (`minX`) or side-B (`maxZ`) face after removing every door span widened by a 0.10 m jamb on both sides. A room facing both sides gets one on each: the control center is glazed at z 6.95–8.15 toward side A and at x 2.10–3.30 toward side B. A room whose longest free run is under 1.20 m gets none — the laundry, whose 3.20 m side-B face is left with only 1.00 m and 1.10 m by its balcony-slab door, and which therefore aerates through that door. Sides C and D are never glazed, so the living room and both kids bedrooms stay on electric light (ADR-006). Eight faces are glazed in all. One rounding choice is recorded: the master bedroom's free run z 0.30–1.75 has odd slack, so the exact centre 0.425 is rounded a centimetre toward +z and the window is z 0.43–1.63; the centring is done in whole centimetres so that the documented rounding rule decides it rather than floating-point noise. Doors: the brief §6 schedule becomes 19 ports as data — 18 doors and the single 3.50 m living-room opening with no leaf — each placed by the span its width covers along its axis, with the Page-2 offsets. Three deviations carry their reason in the data: the master bedroom's corridor door moves 0.02 m west of the drawn x 5.67 to x 5.65, so both its jambs are 0.05 m; the guest room ↔ guest sanitair door moves 0.05 m south of the drawn z 7.05 to z 7.10, because the drawn position cut the sanitair's north wall; and the guest room ↔ kitchen door keeps the z 6.00–6.90 of ADR-006. The two balcony-slab doors stay flush with the void railings as drawn (x 12.70 and x 15.30), and there is deliberately no side-A exterior door. Openings are plain holes from the floor to `heights.door` with a lintel filling the wall above: no glazing and no door leaves until Part 4. Slabs: one per clear rect of every space that has a floor, none for the two voids, with the top at the finished floor level 0 and a derived thickness of `floorToFloor - wall` = 0.30 m, so the slab fills exactly the part of the floor-to-floor height the walls do not and the walls run down through that depth. Lighting: a constant three lights — a hemisphere fill, one sun from side B and a low ambient — present in both views, with per-room light rendered as emissive ceiling panels rather than per-room point lights, because the light count is compiled into every material, so changing it on a view toggle recompiles every shader on the floor. No shadow maps in Part 2; they are revisited in Part 4. Rendering: every box that shares a material is baked into one merged geometry drawn by a single mesh, instead of a component per space, which drops the planned `SpaceModel.tsx`; colours come from a named material palette keyed per surface family and per space kind, so no mesh carries an inline colour. On-screen remote control: each of the eight navigation actions (forward, back, strafe left/right, turn left/right, look up/down) also has a hold-to-act HUD button, held with a pointer, a finger, or Space or Enter while it has focus, with no drag and no gesture anywhere (WCAG 2.5.7) and targets of at least 44 px (WCAG 2.5.8). Keys and buttons meet in one shared domain rule that reduces held actions to a signed intent per axis, so neither input path imitates the other, and the frame loop reads the held buttons through the store's non-reactive `getState()`, so holding a button never re-renders React at 60 fps. A held action must never stick, so it is released by the button's own `pointerup`, `pointercancel` and `lostpointercapture`, by a window-level `pointerup` and `blur`, and by the effect cleanup that also covers leaving the interior view, unmounting and a camera-mode change. After a pointer press focus returns to the 3D view, so the keys keep working; a keyboard hold leaves focus on the button. This closes the single-pointer half of ADR-002 for the interior view, while the exterior orbit stays pointer-drag only until Part 3, so ADR-002 stays accepted. Interim walking limit: the whole floor renders, but walking stays clamped to the master bedroom until Part 3's wall collision, and both that clamp and the third-person follow-camera box are now derived from the floor plan in floor coordinates, replacing the origin-centred demo chamber of ADR-004.
- alternatives: keeping the elevator as a blocked shaft (the owner dropped it, and with it went the reason ADR-006 had for showing the stairs as a block at all); a single straight flight (17 risers need a 4.00 m run, the flight area offers 2.40 m, and the flight would have stopped at 1.59 m instead of 3.00 m); a spiral or winder stair (its treads fall below the going at the walking line, and the bay is a plain rectangle that suits a dog-leg); three flights with two quarter-landings (two flights of 0.75 m already fill the 1.50 m depth of the bay); blocking the whole bay including the landing (the landing is the only way onto the floor, and brief §4.2 makes it continuous with the corridor); full wall height everywhere (a 2.70 m wall along the A balcony and the void would wall off the open side and darken the rooms that depend on it); railings everywhere a floor meets open air (every other void edge already carries a 0.30 m weather-facing wall, so a rail there would be a second guard in front of a parapet); counting railings in the brief §8 wall area (they are rail profiles standing on floor area already counted); placing one window per facade without removing the door spans (the master bedroom's window would have overlapped its balcony door); giving the laundry a narrower window so that every room has one (it would break the single window size the owner confirmed in ADR-006); centring windows in metres (the half-centimetre slack of the master bedroom's run would let floating-point noise pick the rounding); drawing each window and door by hand from Page-2 (a room or a wall that moves would silently leave its opening behind); door leaves and glazing now (Part 4 owns the fixtures, and a leaf hides whether the hole itself is right); a slab thickness written as its own number (it would drift away from the wall and floor-to-floor heights it must complement); per-room point lights (the light count is baked into every compiled material, so toggling the view would recompile every shader on the floor and stall the first frame); shadow maps in Part 2 (cost and shadow acne over hundreds of wall blocks, with no fixtures yet to cast anything worth seeing); one component per space, the planned `SpaceModel.tsx` (hundreds of draw calls, and each space would carry its own inline colours); inline colours per mesh (a palette change would mean editing every mesh, and Leva could not override a family by name); keyboard-only navigation (the owner cannot always reach a keyboard, which is what prompted the request); a drag joystick or click-to-move (a joystick is exactly the dragging movement WCAG 2.5.7 rules out, and click-to-move needs the collision model Part 3 brings); reading the held buttons with a store hook in the frame loop (it would re-render the camera component 60 times a second); releasing a held action only on the button's own `pointerup` (a pointer released off the button, a lost capture or an Alt-Tab would leave a turn spinning forever); waiting for Part 3's collision before rendering the floor at all (the owner wants to see the built floor now, and a one-room clamp is the smaller compromise); extending the interim clamp room by room (it would grow into a second, competing collision model).
- consequences: ADR-006 stays accepted, and this ADR supersedes only its stairs-and-elevator placeholder and closes its open item on the arrival area; Part 3 takes its interior start pose from the stairs arrival rather than from a corner of a chamber. Because 17 risers divide 3.00 m into 0.17647… m, vertical levels are deliberately not snapped to the centimetre plan grid, unlike every plan coordinate: rounding a tread top would either lose the last step or overshoot the slab. The flight area is handed to Part 3's collision as a blocked rect, so the stairs are seen and climbed visually but not walked up. The living room and both kids bedrooms still receive no daylight, which keeps the ADR-006 open item and its tension with brief §1 alive, and the laundry now depends on its balcony-slab door for air. Railings are excluded from the 42.52 m² wall figure, so any later area report must not add them in. Walls that run down to −0.30 m keep every doorway with a threshold and every section closed, at the cost of geometry below the finished floor that is only visible from outside or in section. Openings without leaves or glass mean the interior is closed visually but not physically, and Part 4 adds both. One merged geometry per material means that changing a single box rebuilds that whole geometry, and the component that owns it must dispose it; a Leva override applies to a whole family at once by design. Emissive ceiling panels glow without illuminating anything, so a room lit only by its panel reads as lit rather than being lit, which is acceptable while there are no fixtures and is revisited with the Part 4 lighting pass. The remote control makes the interior view fully operable with a single pointer, so ADR-002's gap is closed for the interior only; the exterior orbit is still drag-and-wheel, and the Part 3 ADR is the one that supersedes ADR-002. Finally, the whole floor is visible while only the master bedroom is walkable, which reads as an invisible wall at that room's doorways until Part 3 lands; `interimWalkArea.ts` and `INTERIM_WALK_SPACE_ID` are deleted then rather than extended.

## ADR-007 — Third-person view with a 1.80 m person (2026-09-11)

- status: accepted
- context: The owner asked to walk the chamber in third person as well as in first person. ADR-004's eye height of 1.60 m was provisional, and ADR-006 records the owner's answer that eye height follows a 1.80 m person at 1.68 m. A camera following a person indoors has to keep the body in view inside a 5.00 × 3.40 m chamber under a 2.70 m wall height, where a wall is often close behind the person (every entry starts with the back to a corner), and the switch between the two views needs the same keyboard scoping as the interior keys (WCAG 2.1.4) plus a single-pointer alternative.
- decision: Person and eye height: the person is 1.80 m tall with eyes at 1.68 m, in `PERSON_SPEC` (`domain/person.ts`). Eye height moves out of `FLOOR_HEIGHTS`, because it is a size of the person, not of the building; this replaces ADR-004's 1.60 m. Follow camera: the pure domain function `getThirdPersonCamera` places the camera about 2.5 m behind the head, 15° above it at level pitch, looking at the head, and turns it with the person. I and K tilt it within 0°–80° of elevation: looking fully up gives a level view from behind, never a view from below the head, so the camera never clips the body. The camera is pulled in along its ray to stay 0.15 m inside the chamber box (the clear rect and the floor-to-ceiling range). Rise near walls: when the pull-in leaves less than 0.5 m, the camera rises toward overhead to the lowest elevation that keeps 0.5 m, limited by the ceiling, so the body stays visible even with its back to a wall or a corner. The mannequin is hidden only if even that fails. Mannequin: a low-poly procedural figure whose parts are named ratios of the person's height, flat shaded, visible in third person only. Switch: V (physical `KeyV`, only while the "Interior 3D view" region has focus, with no modifiers and no key repeat) and a HUD toggle button "Third person" with `aria-pressed`, plus the status line "View: Interior · First person" or "View: Interior · Third person". A pointer click on the button returns focus to the view, so the keys keep working; keyboard activation keeps focus on the button. The last choice is kept across exterior ↔ interior changes, and the default is first person. The pose is stepped once per frame (frame priority −1) and shared by the camera and the mannequin.
- alternatives: a high chase camera with the walls cut away (the owner chose pulling the camera in); a top-down follow camera; a shoulder offset (at corners there are walls on both sides); a different start pose (it fixes only the entry, not every wall the person backs up to); hiding the mannequin near walls (third person then looked like first person); letting the camera go below the head (it clips the torso and flips from above to below the head near walls); easing the camera between positions (not needed once the elevation stays at or above 0°); pointer-lock mouse look (rejected in ADR-004).
- consequences: The chamber-box pull-in is replaced by wall collision in Part 3. Close to walls the view is near-overhead and shows mostly the head and shoulders. The first frame showing the mannequin compiles its shaders, which takes seconds in software WebGL; the end-to-end tests allow for it. Some screen readers (iOS VoiceOver, TalkBack) send pointer clicks, so focus also returns to the view there; this is acceptable because the view precedes the button in the Tab order and is labelled. ADR-004 stays accepted; only its eye height is superseded.

## ADR-006 — Owner answers: typical apartment floor, entry, openings and heights (2026-09-11)

- status: accepted
- context: Part 1 of `TASKS.md` collected the questions that the brief and drawing Page-2 leave open or answer in conflicting ways: whether the plan is a house or a building floor, where the floor is entered, which doors the bedrooms get, the width of the link-corridor door to the A balcony, how the rooms on blocked side C get light, the thickness of the wall between the east void and the utility room, the sanitair sub-room layout, the vertical sizes, the window size and sill, how the stairs and elevator are shown, and where the app and its repository live. Page-2 also draws the guest room ↔ kitchen door over the guest-sanitair wall. ADR-003 rules that such gaps and conflicts go to the owner instead of being guessed; the owner answered them on 2026-09-11.
- decision: Precedence: owner answers recorded in an ADR win over the brief, and the brief still wins over drawing Page-2 (ADR-003). Framing: the plan is the unified typical floor (floors 1…N) of an apartment building; floor 0 is not designed yet. The app shows floor 1 only, placed at level 0 (y = 0), and the stairs and elevator are the building's real vertical circulation. This amends ADR-003's one-storey reading. Entry: the floor is entered through the stairs only for now. The side-A exterior wall has no door, and the A balcony is private, reached through the master bedroom and link corridor doors. This overrides brief §1 (main door on side A), the §5.1 role "Entry arrives here first" and the §6 row "Side A (exterior) → A balcony — main entry"; Page-2 draws no main entry either, even though its legend defines one. Doors: the master bedroom opens onto the corridor and the A balcony, and the male and female kids bedrooms get one corridor door each, which resolves the §6 row "Bedrooms (3) — corridor only". The link corridor ↔ A balcony door is 0.80 m as drawn, a stated exception to the 0.90 m default: the link corridor is 0.90 m clear, so a 0.90 m door would leave no jambs. The guest room ↔ kitchen door sits at z 6.00–6.90 in the wall at x 9.80–10.00, shifted 0.15 m north of the drawn z 6.15–7.05, which overlapped the guest-sanitair north wall (z 6.90–7.10); the owner may move it later. Light: the living room and both kids bedrooms touch only blocked side C, so no daylight is possible. They get electric light only, an air system comes later, the plan is not changed, and the item stays open. Walls: the wall between the east void and the utility room stays 0.20 m (x 20.20–20.40), as drawn and as the brief's width chain requires, a stated exception to brief §2's 0.30 m rule for walls facing the void. Heights: floor-to-floor 3.00 m, wall 2.70 m, door 2.10 m and railing 1.10 m are confirmed. Eye height follows a 1.80 m person at 1.68 m and is delivered with ADR-007 (third-person view). Windows: 1.20 × 1.20 m with a 0.90 m sill (head 2.10 m), the same size everywhere, on side A and side B faces only. Stairs and elevator: blocked placeholder volumes (an elevator shaft box and a stair block), not walkable, because a straight stair rising 3.00 m does not fit in 3.70 m beside an elevator. Sanitairs: the main sanitair is `[open sink [shower][bath]]` and the guest sanitair is `[open sink [bath]]`. The shower and bath sub-rooms sit on the void (side B) side for ventilation, and the open sink area reaches the void only through them. Sub-room sizes and inner door widths are proposed in Part 4 for owner approval. Hosting: self-hosted; the concrete target and deploy workflow are decided in Part 4. Repository: the public GitHub repo `el-amin-dev/building`. Work flows issue → branch `<type>/<issue>-<slug>` → pull request → green CI → squash merge, and `main` is protected.
- alternatives: keep the one-storey house reading (the stairs and elevator would stay reserved space with no purpose, and floor 0 and stacking would have no frame); a street door in the side-A wall (the owner wants the A balcony private, and Page-2 draws no such door); a 0.90 m link door (it would fill the whole 0.90 m link corridor width and leave no jambs); a 0.30 m wall between the east void and the utility room (it shrinks the utility room below its brief §4.3 width of 1.80 m to keep the 22.50 m width chain); the guest room ↔ kitchen door at its drawn position (it cuts into the guest-sanitair wall); walkable stairs (a straight flight does not fit the 3.70 m bay beside the elevator); resolving each conflict during implementation (untracked guesses would end up hard-coded in domain data, which ADR-003 rules out).
- consequences: ADR-003 stays accepted and its text is unchanged; this ADR amends its one-storey reading and supplies the window size it was waiting for. The provisional heights of ADR-004 are confirmed, except the 1.60 m eye height, which ADR-007 (third-person view) replaces. Part 2 builds the port schedule with no side-A door, entry via the stairs, the 0.80 m link door and the guest room ↔ kitchen door at z 6.00–6.90; places 1.20 × 1.20 m windows at a 0.90 m sill on side A and side B faces; shows the stairs and elevator as blocked volumes; and checks reachability from the stairs. Part 3 starts the interior walk at the stairs arrival point, whose position the owner confirms alongside the Part 2 stairs volumes. Part 4 proposes the sanitair sub-room sizes and inner door widths for owner approval, records the self-hosted target and deploy workflow in an ADR, and shows the living room and kids bedrooms as lit by electric light only. The 0.80 m link door and the 0.20 m void wall are explicit exceptions with their reason in domain data (ADR-005), so they are not later "fixed" back to the defaults. The three rooms without daylight stay in tension with the brief §1 note that every habitable room gets light and air from side A or B. Floor 0 and floors 2…N stay deferred.

## ADR-005 — Floor plan coordinates and model (2026-09-11)

- status: accepted
- context: Part 1 turns the whole typical floor (ADR-006) into domain data that the walls, ports, collision and HUD of Parts 2–3 read. The brief gives clear sizes and depth and width chains but no coordinate system, and Page-2 gives positions in pixels. The model needs one frame, one representation for every space (including the L-shaped guest room and the side-B void, which the balcony slab cuts in two), and one rule for the wall thickness between neighbours, and it has to reproduce every brief §8 number.
- decision: Coordinates: the origin is the outer corner where sides A and C meet; x runs along A→D from 0 to 22.50, z along C→B from 0 to 10.00, y points up, and units are metres. The rendered floor sits at y 0. Each space is a set of clear (inner) axis-aligned rects on a centimetre grid, enforced by validation; comparisons use a 1e-9 m tolerance and derived lengths are rounded to the centimetre. A space has one of four kinds: room, circulation, openAir or void. The side-B void is split into `voidWest` (x 1.60–12.70) and `voidEast` (x 16.20–20.20), so every space is one connected region; brief §5.2 already names the two parts. The guest room is the net L: the gross L minus the guest-sanitair block (x 8.00–9.80, z 6.90–8.40, the sanitair's clear rect plus its 0.20 m walls, 2.70 m²). The net shape has two reflex corners with no axis-aligned chord between them, so it is modelled as three rects: x 4.00–8.00 z 6.70–8.40, x 7.20–8.00 z 5.60–6.70, and x 8.00–9.80 z 5.60–6.90. Point lookup is half-open `[min, max)`: a min face belongs to the space and a max face does not, so at a zero-gap join the point goes to the space on the + side. Join thickness follows one rule: `voidFacing` (0.30 m) when either side is openAir or void, otherwise `partition` (0.20 m); the envelope uses `exterior` (0.30 m). Explicit overrides carry their reason: stairsElevator ↔ corridor is 0 (one continuous hall, brief §4.2); voidWest ↔ balconySlabB and voidEast ↔ balconySlabB are 0 (the same open-air strip); voidEast ↔ utilityRoom is 0.20 m (ADR-006). The rule lives in domain data, not only in tests, because Part 2's wall generation reuses it. Areas are not stored in the data; the brief §8 table lives only in the tests as an independent check of the rects. `FLOOR_PLAN` is validated by tests, not at module load. The base chamber spec is derived from the master bedroom clear rect and keeps the uniform 0.20 m walls of ADR-004.
- alternatives: origin at the plan centre (every brief chain starts at an outer face, so each position would need an offset and negative values); integer centimetre storage (exact, but every reader converts to metres, and the values no longer read like the brief; a validated centimetre grid in metres gives the same exactness); a single void space (the balcony slab splits it, so it would be two disconnected regions); the gross L with the guest sanitair overlapping it (it breaks the no-overlap check and point lookup, and the net area of 10.02 m² would exist only as a subtraction); gap expectations only in tests (Part 2 would have to repeat the rule to generate walls).
- consequences: Walls, ports and collision in Parts 2–3 use these coordinates and the join rule. The side-B balcony slab (x 12.70–16.20) faces only x 14.20–16.20 of the laundry's 3.20 m side-B wall (x 14.20–17.40) and x 12.70–14.00 of the kitchen, so Part 2's laundry ↔ slab and kitchen ↔ slab doors must lie within those ranges; drawing Page-2 already places them at x 15.30–16.20 and x 12.70–13.60. A point exactly on a max face returns no space unless another space starts there with a zero gap, so callers treat "no space" as a normal result. Any new zero-gap or non-default join needs an explicit override with its reason. Because `FLOOR_PLAN` is not validated at module load, an invalid edit fails the test suite, not the running app. The §8 areas are checked but not available as data, so a later area display (Part 4 room info panel) computes them from the rects. The base chamber keeps its 0.20 m demo walls until the full floor replaces it.

## ADR-004 — Interior eye navigation with scoped keyboard controls (2026-09-11)

- status: accepted
- context: Part 0 needs a way to walk inside one chamber, the 5.00 × 3.40 m clear base shared by the master and kids bedrooms, with an eye-level camera. ADR-002 deferred keyboard and single-pointer camera control to this navigation work. The interior controls have to meet WCAG 2.1.1 Keyboard, 2.1.4 Character Key Shortcuts (single-character shortcuts must be scoped, remappable or able to be turned off) and 2.5.7 Dragging Movements.
- decision: In the interior view, W A S D move (forward, back, strafe left, strafe right), J L turn left and right, and I K look up and down. Keys are bound by physical `KeyboardEvent.code`, so they keep their position on any keyboard layout. There is no pointer lock and no mouse-drag look, so the interior needs no dragging movement. The keys are scoped to the canvas wrapper, a focusable `role="application"` region named "Interior 3D view" (distinct from the "Interior view" toggle button, so voice control commands are unambiguous). The HUD shows the short hint "Move: W A S D · Look: I J K L"; the region's accessible description spells out each key, states that keys follow physical QWERTY positions, and says that Tab moves to the view toggle. It takes focus when the interior view opens, shows a visible focus outline, and handles keys only while it has focus, which satisfies 2.1.4 (shortcuts active only when the component has focus). Ctrl, Alt and Meta combinations are ignored, and held keys clear when focus leaves the region, the window blurs, or Meta (Cmd) is pressed or released (macOS browsers send no keyup for other keys while Cmd is held). Movement is the pure domain function `stepEyePose`: walk 1.4 m/s, turn 90°/s, look 60°/s, frame step capped at 0.1 s, diagonal movement normalised, position clamped to the clear rectangle shrunk by a 0.25 m body radius, pitch limited to ±80°. Each entry starts in the +x/+z corner of that rectangle facing the opposite corner, so two walls, the corner, floor and ceiling are in view. It is unit-tested without WebGL. The per-frame pose lives in a React ref inside `EyeCameraControls` (Euler order `YXZ`), not in the Zustand store. The chamber uses uniform 0.20 m walls as a deliberate demo simplification: brief §2 "Wall specification" and the drawing use 0.30 m for exterior and void-facing walls, and the full floor model in Part 1 replaces the base chamber spec. The rectangle clamp is replaced by wall collision in Part 3. On-screen hold-to-act buttons are not built yet (Part 3).
- alternatives: arrow keys (they conflict with screen reader browse keys and page scrolling, and one hand cannot combine moving and looking as easily as with two letter clusters); pointer-lock mouse look (needs a pointer, is hostile to keyboard and assistive technology users, and relies on continuous drag-like pointer movement); keeping the pose in the Zustand store (every subscriber would re-render on every frame).
- consequences: ADR-002 is only partly addressed. The interior view is keyboard operable; the exterior orbit is still pointer-only, and single-pointer (button) controls for every camera movement stay open until Part 3, whose ADR supersedes ADR-002. ADR-002 therefore stays accepted. Pointer-only users can switch to the interior view but cannot move in it until the Part 3 buttons exist. Letter keys act only while the view has focus, so after using the HUD a user returns focus to the view before walking. The 0.20 m walls and the single-rectangle clamp are temporary geometry that Parts 1 and 3 replace. The eye pose is not shared state, so a future HUD element that shows the position (for example a minimap) needs a deliberate way to read it. The vertical sizes in `FLOOR_HEIGHTS` (floor-to-floor 3.00 m, wall 2.70 m, door 2.10 m, railing 1.10 m, eye 1.60 m) are provisional: they come from the roadmap, not from the owner brief, which gives no vertical sizes, and the owner confirms them in Part 1 of `TASKS.md` ("confirm unified heights").

## ADR-003 — Sources of truth for the floor model (2026-09-11)

- status: accepted
- context: The 3D floor is built from two owner-authored files that overlap and do not always agree: `docs/house-design-brief.md`, the owner's written requirements (site and orientation, wall specification, room program with exact inner dimensions, open-air elements, door schedule, fixtures, area verification, open items), and `docs/source-of-truth-n-floor.drawio.html`, a draw.io drawing whose pages include earlier layouts. Domain data, tests and geometry need one rule for which file and which page decides a value, and neither file says which one wins.
- decision: The brief wins over the drawing on any conflict. In the drawing, Page-2 is authoritative and Page-4 is history only; drawing scale is 30 px = 1 m. Page-2 gives an envelope of 22.50 × 10.00 m, exterior walls of 0.30 m and interior partitions of 0.20 m (per its legend text), matching brief §2 "Wall specification". The house is modelled as one storey (brief §1 "Site & orientation"): the stairs and elevator are space reserved for future floors, not a second level. No windows are modelled yet, because their size and sill height await owner answers.
- alternatives: the drawing wins (it is precise geometry, but it is an earlier artefact and the brief's §10 "History of changes" records decisions made after some drawn layouts); resolve each conflict case by case during implementation (untracked guesses would end up hard-coded in domain data); model the latest page of the drawing (Page-4 is superseded history, not the current plan).
- consequences: A value is read from the brief first and taken from Page-2 only where the brief is silent. Conflicts between the brief and the drawing, and gaps in both (for example window size and sill), are resolved by the brief or recorded as owner questions, never guessed. Both files are excluded from Prettier in `.prettierignore` so they stay as the owner authored them; the only change made before the first commit removed a one-line handling note at the top of the brief, which carried no requirement. Windows, and any value that depends on them, stay out of the model until the owner answers.

## ADR-002 — Defer keyboard and single-pointer camera navigation (2026-09-11)

- status: accepted
- context: The placeholder scene moves the camera only through drei `OrbitControls`: drag to orbit, wheel or pinch to zoom. Keyboard users cannot move the camera, which fails WCAG 2.1.1 Keyboard, and orbiting needs a dragging movement with no single-pointer alternative, which fails WCAG 2.5.7 Dragging Movements. Camera navigation is the first real feature to be built, so controls made now for a placeholder box would be reworked once the real navigation model (exterior orbit, interior movement between floors and rooms) exists.
- decision: Defer accessible camera navigation to the camera-navigation feature, which must ship keyboard controls and single-pointer (button) controls for every camera movement before it is done. Until then the canvas carries no misleading role (neither `role="img"`, which hides that it is interactive, nor `role="application"`, which announces keyboard interaction that does not exist), and a visually hidden description states that camera movement is pointer-only for now.
- alternatives: build HUD rotate/zoom buttons and key bindings now (throwaway work before the navigation model is designed); `role="application"` on the canvas (misleads assistive technology users into expecting keyboard support).
- consequences: The placeholder scene is not WCAG 2.2 AA conformant for camera movement until the camera-navigation feature lands. The HUD (view mode toggle) is already keyboard and single-pointer operable.

## ADR-001 — Frontend stack: Vite + React + TypeScript + React Three Fiber (2026-09-11)

- status: accepted
- context: The product is a browser-only 3D building that users explore from the outside and then inside (floors, rooms, kitchen). It needs a declarative scene graph that composes with a regular UI layer (HUD, menus), fast local iteration, unit-testable logic, and an end-to-end check that WebGL actually renders. There is no backend.
- decision: Vite 8 + React 19.2 + TypeScript 6.0; three.js through `@react-three/fiber` 9 and `@react-three/drei` 10; Zustand 5 for shared UI state; Tailwind CSS 4 for the HUD; Leva for a development-only tweak panel; Vitest 5 + Testing Library (jsdom) for unit tests; Playwright (Chromium only) for end-to-end tests; ESLint 10 + Prettier. Code lives in a single feature module `src/features/building` with `domain` (pure TypeScript, no React/three) → `application` (store) → `ui` (components) seams, dependencies pointing inward; environment variables are parsed by a pure function (`src/app/parseAppConfig.ts`) that both the Vite config (fail fast at dev/build start) and the app (`src/app/config.ts`) call. Version caps: `react`/`react-dom` stay on `~19.2.8` because `@react-three/fiber` 9.7 declares the peer range `react >=19 <19.3` (19.3.0 is already published); `typescript` stays on `~6.0.3` because `typescript-eslint` 8.x declares the peer range `typescript <6.1.0` (7.x is already published). `jsdom` stays on `^29.1.1` because jsdom 30 requires Node `^22.22.2 || ^24.15.0 || >=26.0.0`, while 29.x accepts Node `^22.13.0 || >=24.0.0` and works with Vitest 5 (peer range `*`). `engines.node` is `^22.13.0 || ^24.0.0 || >=26.0.0`: the intersection of every `engines.node` range in the resolved dependency graph, bounded by Vitest 5 (`^22.12.0 || ^24.0.0 || >=26.0.0`) and ESLint 10 / jsdom 29 (`^20.19.0 || ^22.13.0 || >=24`).
- alternatives: plain three.js (imperative scene management, no component model shared with the HUD); Babylon.js (heavier runtime, weaker React integration); Next.js (server rendering adds nothing to a client-side WebGL app); Redux Toolkit (more ceremony than Zustand for small UI state); Jest (separate transform pipeline, whereas Vitest reuses the Vite config); Cypress (Playwright is faster headless and simpler to run in CI); latest React 19.3 / TypeScript 7 (outside the peer ranges of `@react-three/fiber` and `typescript-eslint`); jsdom 30 (narrows the supported Node range for no benefit to these tests).
- consequences: React and TypeScript upgrades are blocked until `@react-three/fiber` and `typescript-eslint` widen their peer ranges; the `~` ranges keep `pnpm update` from crossing the caps. Node 22 older than 22.13.0 is unsupported; moving to jsdom 30 later raises the floor to `^22.22.2 || ^24.15.0 || >=26.0.0`. The WebGL `<Canvas>` cannot run under jsdom, so the 3D scene is covered only by the Playwright smoke test. Leva is part of the production bundle (its panel is hidden outside development). three.js makes the main chunk large; code splitting is deferred until real building content exists.
